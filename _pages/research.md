My research asks how the visual system regulates sleep and circadian behaviour, and which neurons carry visual signals to sleep and clock circuits. I use *Drosophila melanogaster* because its genetic tools allow manipulation of single cell types, and its connectome allows the anatomical pathways to be mapped at synaptic resolution.

Visual neurotransmission and sleep
------
Sleep disturbance is reported in children with oculocutaneous albinism and other visual disorders. It is not known whether the genes involved act on sleep directly or through reduced vision alone. In my PhD I tested *Drosophila* orthologues of human genes associated with nystagmus and oculocutaneous albinism for sleep and circadian phenotypes, using RNAi knockdown in the eye followed by null mutants.

The screen identified the *OCA2* orthologues *hoe1* and *hoe2* and the *SLC45A2* orthologue *lovit*. Null mutants of these genes showed reduced sleep, reduced circadian rhythmicity and altered photoreceptor neurotransmission in electroretinogram recordings, without a pigmentation defect. These results indicate a neuronal function of these genes that is separate from melanin synthesis.

* Akhtar M *et al.* (2026). bioRxiv. [doi:10.64898/2026.05.27.728351](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.05.27.728351)

Circuits downstream of photoreceptors
------
Lovit transports histamine into synaptic vesicles in photoreceptors. My current work asks which postsynaptic neurons in the lamina and medulla relay this histamine signal to the neurons that control sleep, and whether the same route reaches the circadian clock. I combine cell-type-specific RNAi, rescue and activity reporters with analysis of the FlyWire connectome. A manuscript on this work is in preparation.

Connectomics of visual projection neurons
------
I analyse the FlyWire and hemibrain connectomes in R to trace pathways from visual projection neurons to central brain and descending neurons. My current analysis concerns lobula columnar (LC) neurons and the routes by which their outputs reach descending neurons that control behaviour.

Earlier work
------
During my MS and as a research assistant at COMSATS University Islamabad, I analysed the genome of *Pseudomonas koreensis* WZ005 to identify candidate nematicidal genes ([NCBI BioProject PRJNA719469](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/bioproject/PRJNA719469)) and developed a DpnI-assisted methylome profiling (DAMP) method. Before that, I studied the association of *CCR6*, *AFF3* and microRNA gene variants with rheumatoid arthritis in a Pakistani cohort, and characterised coding and regulatory variants in *CCR6* and *TOX3* in silico. These studies are listed on the [Publications](/publications/) page.

Methods
------
* **Genetics:** GAL4/UAS, split-GAL4, RNAi, genetic rescue, null and hypomorphic alleles, multi-transgene cross design
* **Behaviour:** sleep and circadian analysis with the *Drosophila* Activity Monitor system
* **Physiology and imaging:** electroretinography, CaLexA activity reporting, immunohistochemistry, HCR RNA-FISH, confocal microscopy
* **Molecular biology:** PCR, qRT-PCR, cloning, ELISA, SDS-PAGE
* **Computation:** R, MATLAB, Python, GraphPad Prism, ImageJ; FlyWire, neuPrint, FlyBase, DIOPT
