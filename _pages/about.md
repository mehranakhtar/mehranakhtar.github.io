I am a *Drosophila* neuroscientist. I am interested in the neural circuits that process sensory information, visual information in particular, and convert it into behaviour. I want to understand how a sensory signal is transformed as it passes from sensory neurons through central circuits, and how that transformed signal selects and regulates a behavioural output.

I completed my PhD in Genetics at the University of Leicester in 2026, in the Chen Lab, Division of Genetics and Genome Biology. My supervisors were Dr Ko-Fan Chen, Dr Mervyn Thomas and Professor Flaviano Giorgini. My thesis is titled *Lovit protein in photoreceptors regulates histamine-mediated* Drosophila *sleep and circadian rhythm*.

In my PhD I screened *Drosophila* orthologues of human genes associated with nystagmus and oculocutaneous albinism for sleep and circadian phenotypes. The screen identified *hoe1*, *hoe2* and *lovit* as genes required for sleep and circadian rhythmicity ([Akhtar et al., 2026, bioRxiv](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.05.27.728351)). *lovit* encodes a vesicular histamine transporter in photoreceptors. This work showed that a change in photoreceptor neurotransmission alters a behaviour that is not itself visually guided. I now use fly genetics, behaviour and connectome analysis to trace the neurons downstream of photoreceptors that carry this signal, and to map pathways from visual projection neurons to the descending neurons that control movement.

Before my PhD, I worked on the genetics of rheumatoid arthritis in a Pakistani cohort, in silico analysis of human coding and regulatory variants, and comparative genomics of a nematicidal bacterium.

I am seeking a postdoctoral position in *Drosophila* circuit or systems neuroscience.
